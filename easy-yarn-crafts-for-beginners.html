Easy Yarn Crafts for Beginners: 10 Simple Projects to Try

A small amount of leftover yarn can become something useful, colorful, or lovely to display. If you are new to yarn crafts, start with projects that use simple wrapping, tying, braiding, or basic weaving. You do not need a large collection of tools, and most of these ideas can be adapted to the yarn you already have.

This guide covers ten approachable ideas, what you need for each one, and a few practical details that help the finished project look neat. The projects use different techniques, so choose the one that matches the supplies and time you have.

Before You Start: Choose Yarn and Gather a Few Tools

Medium-weight yarn is a convenient starting point for many small decorative projects because it is easy to handle and widely available. Smooth acrylic or cotton yarn works for many of the ideas below, but the best choice depends on the result you want. Check the yarn label for fiber content and care instructions.

  • Small amounts of yarn in one or more colors
  • Sharp craft scissors
  • A ruler or measuring tape
  • Cardboard for wrapping yarn
  • A blunt yarn needle for projects that need weaving or finishing
  • Optional: a small piece of cardboard, a simple weaving frame, or a suitable ring
Helpful tip: Make a small test piece first when size matters. Yarn thickness and how tightly you wrap or tie it can change the final dimensions.

10 Easy Yarn Crafts for Beginners

1. Classic Yarn Pom-Poms

Pom-poms are one of the simplest ways to use short lengths of yarn. Wrap yarn evenly around a piece of cardboard or a pom-pom maker until it looks full. Tie a separate strand tightly around the middle, slide the loops off, cut through the loops on both sides, and trim the strands into a round shape. Make a few in different sizes for a garland, gift topper, or room decoration.

2. Simple Yarn Tassels

Wrap yarn around a small book or piece of cardboard. Slide the bundle off carefully, tie a strand near one end to form the tassel head, then cut the loops at the opposite end. Add another wrap below the head for a clean finish. Tassels work well on bookmarks, bags, key rings, and wall hangings.

3. A Braided Yarn Bookmark

Cut three equal lengths of yarn, knot them together at one end, and secure the knot under a book or clip. Braid the strands until the bookmark is long enough for your book, then knot the end. Trim the tails evenly or add a small tassel. Keep the braid flat rather than pulling it too tightly.

4. Yarn-Wrapped Letters

Use a sturdy cardboard letter or a plain paper-mâché shape. Add a small amount of craft glue to the back, press the yarn end in place, and wrap the yarn around the shape in close rows. Change colors by securing the old strand on the back and starting the next one there. Keep the wraps snug without bending the letter.

5. A Mini Yarn Wall Hanging

Tie lengths of yarn onto a smooth wooden dowel or a suitable craft stick using simple lark’s-head knots. Arrange the colors in stripes, then trim the lower ends into a straight line or gentle V shape. This is a beginner-friendly decorative weaving-style project, not a crochet pattern. Use a secure hanging method appropriate for the weight of your finished piece.

6. A Colorful Yarn Garland

Make several pom-poms or tassels and attach them at even intervals along a longer piece of yarn or cord. Lay the pieces out before tying them in place so you can adjust the spacing. A garland can decorate a shelf, craft corner, or party table. Keep hanging decorations out of reach of young children and pets.

7. Yarn-Coiled Cardboard Coaster

Cut a sturdy cardboard circle and wrap it with yarn, adding small dots of craft glue as you go. Keep each row close to the previous one so the base is covered. Let the glue dry fully before using it as a decorative mat. This is best treated as a light-duty decorative coaster, not a washable or heat-resistant kitchen item.

8. A Wrapped Pencil Holder

Start with a clean, empty cylindrical container with smooth edges. Secure the yarn at the bottom with craft glue, then wrap upward in neat rows. Change colors for stripes or a simple block pattern. Allow the glue to dry completely and check that no sharp edges remain before placing pencils inside.

9. A Yarn Flower for Decoration

Make a small pom-pom or a few yarn loops for the petals, then tie them securely at the center. Arrange the loops into a flower shape and trim them evenly. Attach the flower to a greeting card, gift wrap, or a decorative clip. If you use a button or small embellishment, keep it away from small children.

10. A Beginner Crochet Chain Bracelet

If you already have a crochet hook, a simple chain is a gentle way to practice holding the hook and controlling yarn. Make a foundation chain long enough to fit loosely around your wrist, then fasten off and weave in the ends with a yarn needle. Check the fit before securing it, and avoid making it tight. For more help with the basics, see our guide on how to hold a crochet hook.

How to Make Your First Yarn Craft Look Neater

  • Keep tension consistent: Try to wrap or braid with similar pressure from start to finish.
  • Hide joins: Secure yarn ends on the back or underside where possible.
  • Trim gradually: Remove small amounts at a time rather than cutting a pom-pom or tassel too short.
  • Test adhesives: Glue can behave differently on cardboard, wood, and fabric. Test a hidden area first and follow the product label.
  • Store leftovers: Keep short lengths sorted by color in a small bag or container for future projects.

Common Beginner Questions

What can I make with leftover yarn?

Try pom-poms, tassels, bookmarks, small garlands, wrapped letters, or decorative flowers. Choose a project that suits the length and amount of yarn you have.

What is the easiest yarn craft to start with?

Pom-poms and tassels are good first projects because they use simple wrapping and tying rather than stitches. A cardboard base and scissors are often enough to begin.

Do I need a crochet hook for yarn crafts?

No. Many yarn crafts use wrapping, braiding, knotting, or simple weaving. A crochet hook is only needed for projects that use crochet stitches, such as the chain bracelet above.

Which yarn is best for beginner crafts?

A smooth, medium-weight yarn is a practical starting point for many small projects. Choose a fiber and thickness that suit the craft, and check the label for care information.

Can children make yarn crafts?

Many simple crafts can be adapted for children with close adult supervision. Adults should handle sharp scissors, needles, hot tools, and small parts, and select materials appropriate for the child's age.

Start Small and Use What You Have

The best first project is one you can finish without buying a lot of supplies. Pick one idea, gather a few colors, and focus on neat wrapping and secure ends. Once you are comfortable with the basics, you can combine pom-poms, tassels, braids, and simple crochet into more personal designs.
