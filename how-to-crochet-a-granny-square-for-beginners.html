How to Crochet a Granny Square for Beginners: Step-by-Step Tutorial, Stitches, Tips & Easy Variations

A granny square is one of those crochet projects that looks intricate but is built from a small set of stitches repeated around a center. Once you learn the rhythm, you can make individual squares for blankets, cushion covers, bags, scarves, and other handmade projects. This beginner-friendly granny square crochet tutorial explains the supplies, stitches, and steps in plain language, with tips to help your square stay flat and neat.

What Is a Crochet Granny Square?

A granny square is a small crochet motif, usually worked in rounds from the center outward. A traditional design uses groups of double crochet stitches separated by chain spaces. The chain spaces create the open, lacy look, while the corner spaces allow the square to grow evenly. You can keep every round the same color or change colors to make a bright patchwork design.

Supplies You Need for an Easy Granny Square

  • Yarn: Smooth, medium-weight yarn is a practical choice for learning because the stitches are easy to see.
  • Crochet hook: Start with the size recommended on your yarn label. Your tension may mean you need to adjust it.
  • Scissors: Use them to cut yarn when changing colors or finishing.
  • Yarn needle: Use a blunt-tipped needle to weave in yarn tails.
  • Stitch marker (optional): Helpful for marking the beginning of a round.

For your first simple crochet square pattern, choose a light or medium shade that makes the stitches easy to see. Very fuzzy or dark yarn can make it harder to identify the chain spaces while you are learning.

How to Crochet a Granny Square: Step by Step

Step 1: Make a Center Ring

Begin with a magic ring, also called a magic circle, or make a short chain and join it into a ring with a slip stitch. A magic ring lets you pull the center closed for a neater middle. If the magic ring feels tricky, the chain-ring method is a perfectly reasonable way to practise your first square.

Step 2: Work the First Round

Chain 3 to count as the first double crochet in this example. Work 2 more double crochet stitches into the center ring to make your first cluster of 3 double crochet. Chain 2 to form a corner. Work another cluster of 3 double crochet into the ring, then chain 2 again. Repeat until you have four clusters, with a chain-2 space between each cluster. Join the round with a slip stitch at the top of the beginning chain-3.

Remember: In this traditional layout, the four chain-2 spaces are the corners. The groups of 3 double crochet form the sides between them.

Step 3: Start the Second Round

Slip stitch across the top of the stitches until you reach a corner space. In that corner space, work a cluster of 3 double crochet, chain 2, and another cluster of 3 double crochet. This makes the next corner. Chain 1, then move to the next corner space and repeat the same corner group. Continue around all four corners, using chain-1 spaces between the corners. Join the round with a slip stitch.

Step 4: Keep Building the Square

For each new round, work a corner group of 3 double crochet, chain 2, and 3 double crochet into every corner space. Along each side, work one cluster of 3 double crochet into each chain-1 space from the previous round, with chain 1 between clusters. As the square grows, the number of side clusters increases. Keep the corner spaces clear so the square can expand.

Step 5: Check That Your Square Lies Flat

After each round, place the motif on a flat surface without stretching it. If the edges curl upward, your stitches may be too tight or there may not be enough space in the corners. If the edges ripple, your tension may be loose or you may have added too many stitches. Check that each corner has the same stitch group and chain space.

Step 6: Finish the Last Round

When your granny square reaches the size you want, finish the round and join neatly with a slip stitch. Cut the yarn, leaving a tail long enough to weave in. Pull the yarn through the final loop to fasten off. Use a yarn needle to weave the tail through several stitches on the back, changing direction once to help secure it.

How to Change Colors in a Granny Square

Color changes are a simple way to personalize a granny square. To change colors at the end of a round, begin the final joining slip stitch with the old color and complete it with the new color, if that is how your chosen method is written. Alternatively, fasten off and join the new yarn in a corner space. Keep the join snug but not tight, and weave in both tails securely. Try two or three colors first so the pattern remains easy to follow.

Tips for Neat, Even Granny Squares

  • Count the four corners after every round.
  • Use the same stitch pattern and corner chain spaces throughout the square.
  • Avoid pulling the yarn too tightly when making chains or slip stitches.
  • Use a stitch marker at the first corner if you lose track of the round.
  • Make two or three practice squares before starting a large blanket.
  • Weave in yarn ends carefully so they do not loosen with use.

Easy Granny Square Variations to Try

Classic Two-Color Granny Square

Use one color for the center and another for the outer rounds. This is a simple way to practise changing yarn without making the pattern complicated.

Scrap-Yarn Granny Square

Use leftover yarn in coordinated colors. Check that the yarns are a similar thickness so the finished square has a consistent fabric and size.

Solid Granny Square

A solid granny square uses a different stitch arrangement with fewer open spaces. It can be a good option when you want a denser fabric for a bag or cushion.

Small Motifs for a Blanket

Make several squares in the same finished dimensions, then arrange them before joining. Our guide to how to crochet a blanket for beginners covers planning a simple blanket and finishing its edges.

Common Granny Square Problems and Simple Fixes

Why is my granny square turning into a circle?

Check that you are making distinct corner spaces and working the same corner group into each one. If the corners are missing or difficult to see, the motif may look rounded rather than square.

Why are the edges wavy?

Wavy edges can come from extra stitches, loose tension, or inconsistent corner groups. Count the clusters on each side and compare the corners from round to round.

Why does my square curl?

Curling can happen when the stitches are too tight or the corner spaces are too small. Try a slightly larger hook or relax your tension, then make another sample to compare.

Can beginners make granny squares?

Yes. If you know how to chain, make a slip stitch, and work a double crochet, you have the main skills needed for a traditional granny square. Practise slowly and count the clusters as you go.

How many granny squares do I need for a blanket?

The number depends on the size of each square and the finished blanket dimensions. Measure one blocked square, then estimate how many fit across and down. Make a few extra if you want flexibility when arranging colors.

What Can You Make With Granny Squares?

Once you are comfortable with the basic motif, you can join squares to make a blanket, cushion cover, tote bag, scarf, or table runner. You can also make a single square into a coaster or decorative patch, depending on the yarn and size. Keep the first project small so you can practise joining and finishing before committing to a large piece.

Final Thoughts

Learning how to crochet a granny square is a useful step for anyone building confidence with crochet. Start with one color and a simple four-corner pattern, focus on keeping your clusters consistent, and check that the square lies flat after each round. When the basic pattern feels familiar, experiment with color changes and different yarn combinations. A few small squares can become the beginning of a thoughtful handmade project.
