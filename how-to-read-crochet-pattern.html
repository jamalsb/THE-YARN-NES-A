How to Read a Crochet Pattern for Beginners

By The Yarn Nest • October 28, 2024

Starting your first crochet project is exciting, but opening a pattern and seeing a bunch of letters like "ch 2, sc in next st" can be terrifying. Don't worry! Crochet patterns are like a secret code, and once you learn the basics, it becomes very easy.

In this guide, I will teach you how to read a crochet pattern step-by-step.

1. Understand the Abbreviations

Crochet patterns use abbreviations to save space. Here are the most common ones you will see:

  • ch: Chain
  • sc: Single Crochet
  • dc: Double Crochet
  • sl st: Slip Stitch
  • st: Stitch

2. Read the Pattern Row by Row

Patterns are read from the bottom up, row by row (or round by round if you are working in a circle). Look for the numbers at the start of each line. For example:

Row 1: Ch 10, sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. (9 sts)

This means: Make 10 chains, then make a single crochet in the second chain from your hook, and then single crochet in every remaining chain. You should have 9 stitches at the end.

3. Pay Attention to Repeats

Sometimes a pattern will tell you to repeat a section. It will usually be written inside asterisks like this: *sc in next st, ch 1, skip next st; repeat from * to end. This means you do that action over and over until you reach the end of the row.

Final Thoughts

Don't be afraid to grab a pencil and circle the numbers as you go. Practice makes perfect, and soon you will be reading patterns like a pro!
