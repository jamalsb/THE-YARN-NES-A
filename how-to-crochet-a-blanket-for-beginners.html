How to Crochet a Blanket for Beginners: A Step-by-Step Guide

A handmade crochet blanket can be a comforting first large project. The key is to keep the design simple, choose a yarn that is comfortable to work with, and make a small sample before you begin. This guide walks through the planning and basic process so you can create a straightforward rectangular blanket at your own pace.

1. Choose a Beginner-Friendly Yarn

For a first blanket, a smooth medium-weight or bulky yarn can be easier to see and handle than very fine yarn. Acrylic yarn is often chosen for its availability and range of colors; cotton has a different feel and drape. Check the yarn label for its recommended hook size, yardage, fiber content, and care instructions. Buy enough yarn from the same dye lot when possible so the colors are consistent.

2. Select a Crochet Hook

Start with the hook size suggested on the yarn label. The right hook also depends on your tension and the fabric you want. If your stitches are very tight and the fabric feels stiff, try a slightly larger hook; if the fabric is loose and has large gaps, try a smaller one. Make a small sample before deciding.

3. Learn the Basic Stitches

A simple blanket can be made with repeated rows of one stitch. If you are new to crochet, practice making a slip knot, a foundation chain, and the stitch you plan to use. Single crochet creates a dense fabric, while double crochet usually makes a taller, more flexible fabric and builds rows faster. Stitch names and abbreviations can vary between US and UK patterns, so check which terminology a pattern uses.

If holding the hook feels unfamiliar, our guide How to Hold a Crochet Hook Properly covers two common grips. You can also read How to Read a Crochet Pattern for Beginners before following a written pattern.

4. Make a Small Gauge Swatch

Make a small square using the yarn, hook, and stitch you plan to use. Count the stitches and rows across a measured section. This sample helps you estimate how wide your blanket will be and whether you like the fabric. If the swatch is too firm or too loose, adjust the hook and make another sample.

Beginner tip: Do not skip the swatch if you want a predictable blanket size. Even a small change in tension can make a large project noticeably wider or narrower.

5. Decide on the Blanket Size

Choose a practical size before starting. A small lap blanket needs less yarn and time than a full bed blanket. Your swatch gives you a rough stitch count: measure how many stitches fit into one inch or centimeter, then multiply by the width you want. Round to a convenient number for your stitch pattern. Remember that borders can add a little width and length.

6. Crochet the Foundation Chain

Make a slip knot and chain the number of stitches calculated for your chosen width. Try not to pull the chain too tightly, because a tight foundation can make the bottom edge narrower than the rest of the blanket. If this happens often, use a slightly larger hook for the foundation chain, then switch back to your main hook for the rows.

7. Work the Rows Evenly

Follow the stitch pattern across the chain. At the end of each row, make the turning chain required for your chosen stitch and turn your work. Count stitches periodically to make sure you have not accidentally added or skipped one. Place a stitch marker in the first or last stitch if it is difficult to see the row edge.

Continue until the blanket reaches your planned length. Keep the yarn tension relaxed and consistent. If you need to join a new ball, do so at the edge when possible and weave in the yarn tails securely with a yarn needle.

8. Add a Simple Border

A border can make the edges look more finished. One beginner-friendly option is to work a round of single crochet around the blanket. Place stitches evenly along the sides, and work a few stitches into each corner so the fabric can lie flat. If the corners curl or ruffle, adjust the number of corner stitches on the next round.

9. Fasten Off and Weave in the Ends

When the blanket is the length you want, fasten off according to your stitch pattern and leave a yarn tail long enough to weave in. Use a yarn needle to pass the tail through several stitches on the back, changing direction once to help secure it. Trim only the excess after the tail is secure. Avoid leaving loose ends that can catch during use.

10. Care for Your Finished Blanket

Follow the care instructions on the yarn label. Different fibers may need different washing and drying methods. If you are unsure, test the care method on your swatch first. Store the finished blanket clean and completely dry.

Common Beginner Questions

What is the easiest crochet stitch for a blanket?

Single crochet and double crochet are both common beginner choices. Single crochet makes a denser fabric; double crochet creates taller rows. Choose the one you find easiest to keep consistent.

How much yarn do I need for a crochet blanket?

It depends on the blanket dimensions, yarn weight, stitch pattern, hook size, and personal tension. Use the yardage estimate on a pattern when available, or make a swatch and calculate from the area of your planned blanket. Buying a little extra from the same dye lot can help avoid a shortage.

Can I crochet a blanket with leftover yarn?

Yes. Leftover yarn can be used for stripes or squares, but try to use yarns with similar thickness and care requirements. A swatch can show whether different yarns create a consistent fabric.

Why are the edges of my blanket uneven?

Uneven edges often happen when a stitch is missed or an extra stitch is added at the row ends. Count your stitches regularly and use a stitch marker to identify the edge stitch.

Should I block a crochet blanket?

Some fibers and stitch patterns benefit from gentle blocking, while others may not need it. Follow the yarn's care guidance and test your chosen method on the swatch before treating the whole blanket.

Enjoy the Process

Your first blanket does not need complicated stitches or color changes. A simple repeated pattern gives you room to practice and see your progress row by row. Start with a manageable size, keep track of your stitch count, and take your time with the finishing.
